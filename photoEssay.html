project abstract:

                                                          This series features photographs taken of art around the city
                                                        in an attempt to document the use of art as a means of resistance.
                                                    Our surroundings inform our world, and our world informs our surroundings.
                                                                      "there is still art there is still hope"

project description:

This project features photographs taken of stickers, graphics, and graffiti found around the city. These images were taken throughout Manhattan; however, the series is
continuously growing. Art is one of the vehicles in which we can enact change, and these photographs display a fraction of the art that aims to spread a message. These
photographs document the multitude of social, political, and environmental issues that are present in society and how people aim to increase exposure to them differently.
Through graphics, plain text, and metaphorical images, each piece demonstrates a microcosm of a larger battle. The creation of art is in and of itself an act of resistance,
and by increasing the visibility of these issues through stickers and graffiti, artists are participating in this act of resistance. 

In addition to the explicit messaging of the art featured in these photographs, these photographs simultaneously present the increase in accessibility to art. Galleries and
museums are no longer the only places in which art can be viewed. The presence of art in what may be considered ordinary and mundane spaces—trash cans, streetlamps, stop signs,
brick walls—reveals the abundance of art in our everyday surroundings. As art surrounds us, so to do the injustices that the same aforementioned art works to combat. Life
informs art; art informs life. The medley of stickers and graffiti on a singular surface represents the intersectionality between all issues and struggles. The deterioration
or defacement of these works reflects societal tension and disagreement. 

Yet, does the abundance of art and stickers and graffiti and political messaging lead to our habituation of these messages? Does the omnipresence of art encourage us to become
ignorant of these small details? Does the duplication of stickers and graphics decrease the pieces' status as art? Ultimately, this project aims to explore the variety in which
art contains messaging and how it conveys these messages.

artist bio:

Maya Estrera (she/her) is an award winning documentary filmmaker and photographer dedicated to sharing stories that change people. She graduated from Swarthmore College with a
B.A. in Film & Media Studies and Environmental Studies in 2025 and is currently pursuing an MFA in Integrated Media Arts at Hunter College. Her most recent project, HYPERSCALED,
unearths the real world cost of AI and data centers in the small community of Bessemer, Alabama. In her free time, she enjoys film photography, DJing, and spending time outdoors.
